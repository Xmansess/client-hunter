## 1. Public reply

- Length: 60 to 150 words. Plain text; short paragraphs or 2 to 4 compact bullets only if the platform renders them.
- Shape: useful core (answer or sharp questions) -> optional honest offer line with disclosure ("I do remote supplier vetting, so I'm biased, but...") -> small next step ("happy to share the checklist I use").
- No links where the community restricts promotion. Never ask people to DM you in communities that ban it.

## 2. DM

- Only when the poster invited DMs, or the ground allows DMs and the request is a hiring post.
- Length: 50 to 110 words. First line references their specific request, not you.
- Shape: one-line relevance -> one insight or question -> what you would do first and roughly what it costs (if the profile has prices) -> small ask (reply with X, or a 15-minute call).
- Sign with the profile signature. Add a portfolio link only if the profile has one.

## 3. Email

- Only when the poster publicly listed an address for this request.
- Subject: specific and short ("Sourcing agent for your smart ring: 3 questions first").
- Body: 80 to 150 words, same shape as the DM, plus where you saw the request.

## 4. Tender cover note

- 120 to 200 words: understanding of the requirement, matching experience from the profile, approach in 3 steps, availability, and the list of documents the tender asks for (checklist for the user).

## 5. Follow-up

- 30 to 70 words, adds one new useful thing, ends with an easy yes or no question. Maximum two per lead.

## 6. Language notes

- Russian: formal "Вы" with businesses and strangers; get to the point quickly; avoid English loanwords where common Russian terms exist.
- Spanish: "usted" for formal B2B and Spain-based companies unless the post is casual; Latin American posts often accept "tú" in casual threads. Match the poster.
- English: plain and direct; no corporate filler.
- Translations for checking go below the draft, labelled clearly, and are faithful rather than polished.

## 7. Worked example

Context: r/smallbusiness, "Looking for a reliable sourcing agent in Shenzhen" for a smart ring brand; replies already include three "DM me" agents. Profile: remote sourcing and supplier evaluation, no on-site visits, honest about it.

Weak (what most replies do):
> Hi! I'm a sourcing agent with 10 years of experience. DM me and I'll help you with everything.

Strong public reply:
> Before choosing anyone, get three things clear, because every agent will ask and they change the answer: your first-order volume, a target landed unit price, and whether the agent may take commission from factories (many do, and then they work for the factory, not you).
>
> For a smart ring, also ask candidates which wearable or BLE products they've taken through prototyping, and who owns the tooling and firmware files.
>
> Disclosure: I do remote supplier search and vetting, so I'm biased. I'm not in Shenzhen, so for factory visits and QC I'd pair with an on-site inspection firm. Happy to share the vetting checklist I use either way.

Why it works: useful without the pitch, specific to wearables, honest about the gap, small next step, no DM begging.
